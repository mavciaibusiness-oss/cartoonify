## Why the message changed

The reasoning is transcribed from the spec (§3) and from the source it cites;
it is not reconstructed here.

The governing argument is the **addendum to finding 11** in
`.mavci/lessons/pending-system-change.md` ("Correction: unreachable does not
mean transient, and the message must not invite a retry"), together with
**finding 12** immediately after it. Its principle: the message should describe
what is known and claim nothing about cause or remedy — "no instruction to check
anything, and no implication that a retry will work."

The operator asked whether that argument reaches `UPSTREAM_ERROR`. Per spec
§3.1, it does, and more strongly than on the path it was written for:

- The addendum was written about `UPSTREAM_UNREACHABLE`, the path where **no
  response arrived at all**. There, transience is genuinely possible — a network
  fault, a momentary DNS failure, a restarting load balancer — so its revised
  message keeps a hedged, permissive "the problem may be temporary; you can try
  again later." The hedge is warranted because the proposition is uncertain.
- `UPSTREAM_ERROR` is the opposite case. After task 0002, this code is reached
  specifically when **the provider answered and refused** — 0002's criterion 15
  holds that line. A refusal is a decision the provider made with the request in
  hand, so re-sending a byte-identical request invites the same evaluation and
  the same answer. The inference "this looks transient, therefore retry" is not
  merely unsupported on this path; it is contradicted by what the path means.
- Finding 12 supplies the concrete cost: the account was out of credit, every
  retry would have failed identically for as long as the balance stayed at zero,
  and each attempt cost 34 seconds.

What is **not** claimed (spec §3.2): that a retry can never succeed here — a
rate limit can expire — and not that the user's image is fine, since a refusal
on an image edit endpoint may concern the uploaded content. Both directions are
unknown, and the wording says so rather than picking one.

The two sibling messages now share one shape, `<what is known>. <what is not
known>; <what follows from that>.`, and differ in the third clause exactly where
the underlying facts differ.
